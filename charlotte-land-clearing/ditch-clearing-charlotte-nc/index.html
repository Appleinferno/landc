Drainage Clearing Know-How

Clearing Brush From Ditches and Swales in Charlotte

How Brush Turns a Working Ditch Into a Dam

A drainage ditch or grassed swale doesn't have to be full of dirt to stop working. Once blackberry canes, privet, and young sweetgum or willow stems sprout in the bottom and along the shoulders, those woody stems catch every pine straw mat, fallen branch, and leaf that washes down during a summer thunderstorm. Within a couple of seasons, water slows down, drops its sediment right in the brush, and spills over the driveway or into the yard.

Clearing Vegetation vs. Regrading the Ditch

We are a brush and land clearing contractor, not an excavation or grading company. We do not dig new ditches, reshape channels with an excavator bucket, or install culvert pipes. What we do is remove the wall of briars, vines, and saplings so water isn't blocked by woody growth—and so you, your landscaper, or your grading contractor can actually see the ditch bottom and culvert ends. On many properties, simply getting the brush out of the channel and off the shoulders restores normal flow.

Working From the Bank, Not Rutting the Bottom

Driving a heavy machine down the wet center of a ditch mashes the banks and leaves deep ruts in the clay. Whenever ground conditions allow, we work from the dry shoulder at the top of the bank and use hand-held saws and brush cutters inside tight or damp channels and around plastic, metal, or concrete culvert pipes. Stems on the bank are cut flush so the root mat continues holding the red clay slope against washouts.

Keeping Debris Out of Culverts

Mulching brush on flat ground is ideal, but leaving heavy wood chips piled inside a narrow ditch channel or right in front of a fifteen-inch driveway pipe will clog the culvert on the next hard rain. Along the upper shoulders we mulch in place, and inside narrow flow paths or around culvert inlets and outlets we cut and pull woody limbs up onto the bank before mulching so the waterway stays clear.

Ditches vs. Regulated Creeks and Stream Buffers

There is an important difference between a man-made roadside or yard drainage ditch and a natural stream or intermittent creek. In Charlotte and Mecklenburg County—as well as in the Catawba and Goose Creek watersheds—natural streams carry regulated undisturbed buffers (such as SWIM and Post-Construction buffers) where clearing vegetation is restricted. During our walk-through we look for stream indicators, stay out of protected creek buffers, and recommend you confirm with the city or county before any work near a mapped stream or public drainage easement.

Setting Up the Ditch for Easy Mowing

The reason most ditches grow over in the first place is that a tangle of briars or a line of volunteer saplings along the top edge keeps you from getting a mower or string trimmer near the bank. By clearing a clean shoulder strip alongside the ditch, we leave it where you can maintain the edge from dry ground.

Questions

Charlotte Ditch & Drainage Clearing FAQs

Do you dig out silt or regrade the ditch bottom?

No. We clear the brush, briars, vines, and saplings growing in and along the ditch. We do not excavate, regrade channels, or install drainage pipe. If your ditch needs dirt work after the vegetation is gone, your grading contractor will have a clean, open channel to work in.

Will mulching the brush clog my driveway culvert?

We don't leave loose wood piles inside the flow channel or in front of culvert pipes. We mulch on the upper shoulders and pull cut stems out of narrow ditch bottoms and pipe openings so water can move cleanly.

Can you clear a creek bank the same way you clear a ditch?

Not always. Natural creeks and intermittent streams in Charlotte and Mecklenburg County are often protected by stream buffer rules that restrict cutting trees and brush near the water. We'll look at the feature during the walk-through, stay out of regulated buffers, and advise you to confirm what's permitted with the city or county.

What about the ditch along the public road in front of my house?

Roadside ditches sit inside the public right-of-way maintained by the city or NCDOT. Property owners often maintain the grass and light brush along their frontage, but before removing larger trees or doing heavy work in a public right-of-way or storm drainage easement, confirm what's allowed with the city or NCDOT.

Will cutting the brush cause the ditch banks to erode?

Pulling stumps out of a ditch bank causes washouts, which is why we cut saplings and brush flush with the ground instead. That leaves the existing root web in the clay to hold the bank while grass and low groundcover fill in.

Can you work around metal or plastic culvert pipes without crushing them?

Yes. We flag culvert ends during the walk-through before any machine moves, keep heavy tracks off thin pipe covers, and clear right around the pipe openings by hand.

How do I keep the ditch from growing back over?

Once we clear a clean strip along the top shoulder of the ditch, running a mower along the shoulder and hitting the slope with a string trimmer a few times a summer will keep sweetgum and privet sprouts from getting woody again.

Do you spray ditches with weed killer?

No. Our clearing is 100% mechanical. We don't use chemical sprays anywhere on your property, especially near water and drainage channels.

How much does ditch and drainage clearing cost in Charlotte?

Price depends on the length of the ditch, how wide and deep the channel is, the size of the saplings, and how much hand cutting is needed in wet bottoms or around culverts. We give you a firm written price after walking the ditch with you.
