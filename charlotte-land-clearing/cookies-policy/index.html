How We Use Cookies: Simple, Honest & Non-Invasive

Land Clearing Charlotte uses cookies, web beacons, and pixels to ensure our website functions correctly, measure how property owners engage with our land clearing guides, and track the effectiveness of our advertising campaigns on Google, Facebook, and Pinterest. We do not sell cookie data to third parties.

1. What Are Cookies and Pixels?

Cookies are small alphanumeric text files stored on your computer, tablet, or smartphone when you visit a website. Pixels (also known as web beacons or tracking tags) are tiny transparent graphics or code snippets embedded on web pages that send data back to advertising platforms when a page is viewed or a form is submitted.

These technologies allow websites to remember your browser preferences, understand which services interest you, and attribute quote requests to specific search queries or advertising campaigns.

3. Why We Rely on These Analytics & Conversion Tags

As a direct service contractor operating specialized heavy equipment, tracking tags are critical to our business in three ways:

  • Cost Control: They prevent us from wasting advertising budget on regions outside our service area (such as outside North Carolina), keeping our overhead low and our estimate pricing competitive.
  • Attribution: When a landowner requests an estimate, conversion tracking helps us know which marketing channel (Google search, Facebook post, or direct inquiry) brought them to our site.
  • Content Relevance: Aggregated analytics guide what articles we write for our Lot Clearing Tips hub based on what Carolina property owners frequently search for (e.g., local permits, erosion control, and per-acre pricing).

4. How You Can Manage, Block, or Opt Out of Cookies

You maintain full control over whether cookies and tracking tags are stored on your device. You can exercise your preferences through several methods:

A. Browser-Level Controls

Most web browsers automatically accept cookies by default, but you can alter your settings to block all cookies, reject third-party cookies, or receive a warning before a cookie is stored. Instructions can be found in your browser's settings menu:

  • Google Chrome: Settings > Privacy and Security > Third-party cookies.
  • Apple Safari: Preferences > Privacy > Prevent cross-site tracking.
  • Mozilla Firefox: Settings > Privacy & Security > Enhanced Tracking Protection.
  • Microsoft Edge: Settings > Cookies and site permissions > Manage and delete cookies.

B. Third-Party Platform Opt-Out Links

You can opt out of behavioral advertising and analytics directly with each provider:

  • Google Analytics Opt-Out: Install the official Google Analytics Opt-out Browser Add-on.
  • Google Ads Personalization: Manage ad settings via the Google Ad Settings Portal.
  • Facebook / Meta Ad Preferences: Adjust ad settings in your Facebook Ad Preferences.
  • Pinterest Privacy Settings: Opt out of personalization under Pinterest Personalization Settings.

C. Industry-Wide Opt-Out Tools

You can also opt out of interest-based advertising from multiple companies simultaneously through self-regulatory industry programs:

  • Digital Advertising Alliance (DAA) WebChoices Tool
  • Network Advertising Initiative (NAI) Consumer Opt-Out

5. "Do Not Track" and Global Privacy Control

Some web browsers broadcast a "Do Not Track" (DNT) or Global Privacy Control (GPC) signal. While there is no universal industry consensus on the technical interpretation of DNT signals, our site respects recognized GPC signals regarding cross-context advertising.
