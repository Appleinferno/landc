Brush Clearing, Done Right

Brush Clearing for Every Kind of Charlotte Property

Overgrown Lot Cleanup

Vacant lots, inherited properties, and backyards that got away from their owners are the most common brush jobs we see in Charlotte. In a few growing seasons, Chinese privet, sweetgum and pine saplings, honeysuckle, and blackberry can turn an open lot into a thicket you can't walk through. We cut everything down to a low, even mulch layer so the lot is open, visible, and easy to maintain with a mower or bush hog afterward.

Code Enforcement & Overgrowth Notices

Charlotte's code enforcement program can issue notices for overgrown vegetation on residential and vacant lots, usually with a deadline to bring the property into compliance. If you've received one, call us with the address and the date on the notice. We'll tell you honestly whether we can meet it and what the property needs. Always check the notice itself for the exact requirements and deadline.

Briars, Vines & Thickets

Briars and vines are the brush most people give up on. Blackberry canes, greenbrier, wild grape, and kudzu-like vine tangles wrap around everything and grow back from the roots. Our tracked mulchers grind the tangle and the saplings holding it up, then we follow up with hand work around fences, trees, and structures where a machine shouldn't go.

Fence Line & Property Line Clearing

A fence line buried in brush is a fence you can't inspect, repair, or replace. We open a clean strip along fences and property lines so a fence crew, surveyor, or neighbor can see exactly where things stand. For new fence installs, we clear a consistent width so the installer has room to work.

HOA Retention & Detention Ponds

Many Charlotte neighborhoods have stormwater ponds that the HOA is responsible for maintaining. When saplings and brush take over the banks and embankment, they can damage the structure and block inspection. We cut back woody growth on pond banks and access paths with equipment sized for slopes and soft ground. Ask your HOA or the city's stormwater program what your pond's maintenance agreement requires before work begins.

Ditches, Drainage Swales & Creek Edges

Overgrown ditches hold water, back up driveways, and flood yards. We clear brush from ditches and swales on private property so water can move again. Creeks are different: Mecklenburg County stream buffer rules limit clearing near many streams, so we'll stop at the buffer line and recommend you confirm what's allowed with the county before any work along a creek.

Keeping Brush From Coming Back

Brush in the Charlotte area grows back fast, especially privet and sweetgum that resprout from the stump. Regular mowing after we clear is the simplest way to keep it in check. For larger areas, a once-a-year or every-other-year cutback is often cheaper than letting it go and paying for a full clearing again. We'll recommend a schedule that fits your property and budget.

What Affects the Cost of Brush Clearing in Charlotte

  • Area size: square footage or acreage being cleared.
  • Brush density and height: waist-high weeds versus head-high privet and saplings.
  • Sapling size: larger stems take more time to grind.
  • Hidden debris: old fencing, tires, or dumped material slow the job.
  • Access and slope: pond banks, ditches, and gated backyards need smaller equipment.
  • Hand work: detail work around fences, sheds, and keeper trees.

We price after seeing the property, so the number you get is the number you pay.

Questions

Charlotte Brush Clearing FAQs

How much does brush clearing cost in Charlotte?

Cost depends on the size of the area, how thick and tall the brush is, the size of any saplings, access, and how much hand work is needed around fences and trees. We give a firm written price after a free walk-through.

I got an overgrowth notice from the city. Can you help?

Yes. Call us with the address and the compliance date on the notice. We'll tell you right away if we can meet it. Check the notice for the exact requirements, since those come from the city, not from us.

What's the difference between brush clearing and land clearing?

Brush clearing targets the undergrowth: weeds, vines, briars, shrubs, and small saplings. Land clearing is a bigger job that can include removing mature trees and preparing ground for construction.

Do you haul the brush away?

In most cases we don't need to. The brush is mulched in place, which leaves a protective layer on the soil and avoids haul-off and dump fees.

Can I just burn the brush myself?

Open burning is heavily restricted in Mecklenburg County, and burning land-clearing debris is generally not allowed. Check with Mecklenburg County Air Quality before burning anything. Mulching on site avoids the issue entirely.

Will the brush grow back?

Some of it will, especially privet and sweetgum, which resprout from the stump. Mowing regularly after clearing keeps it down. For larger areas, we can set up a periodic cutback schedule.

Do you clear HOA retention ponds?

Yes. We cut back saplings and woody brush on pond banks, embankments, and access paths. Check your pond's maintenance agreement or ask the city's stormwater program what's required before work starts.

Can you clear brush along a creek on my property?

Mecklenburg County stream buffer rules limit what can be cleared near many creeks. We'll stop at the buffer and recommend you confirm what's allowed with the county before any work along the stream.

Will you damage my lawn or fence?

We use tracked equipment with low ground pressure and hand-clear right along fences and structures. Access routes are planned before we start so we avoid lawns, septic areas, and soft ground.

When is the best time of year to clear brush in Charlotte?

Any time works, but late fall and winter are ideal. With leaves down, it's easier to see fences, debris, and terrain, and clearing before spring keeps the regrowth from getting a head start.
