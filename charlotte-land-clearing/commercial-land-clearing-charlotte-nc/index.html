Commercial Site Know-How

How We Handle Commercial Clearing in Charlotte

Who We Clear For

Most of our commercial work comes from developers, general contractors, and site contractors who need a dependable clearing sub. Projects range from small retail and office outparcels to townhome and subdivision phases, church and school expansions, and industrial lots along corridors like I-485, I-85, and the airport area. Whether you're the developer holding the permits or the site contractor who needs clearing done before your graders mobilize, we fit into your structure and report to whoever runs the site.

Working to the Approved Plans

Commercial clearing is defined by the plan set, not by what looks right in the field. Before we start, we walk the site with the clearing limits and the limits of disturbance in hand, confirm the stakes or flagging match the drawings, and raise any discrepancies with your engineer or superintendent. If a limit line is unclear in the field, we stop and ask rather than guess. Clearing past an approved boundary is one of the fastest ways to draw a stop-work order, and we won't put your project in that position.

Tree-Save Areas and Charlotte's Tree Ordinance

Charlotte's tree ordinance applies mainly to commercial and new development, which means many commercial sites have tree-save areas, required buffers, or specimen trees shown on the approved plans. We treat those areas as no-go zones. Protective fencing should be up before clearing starts, and our machines stay outside it, including when we're staging material or turning around. Requirements vary by project, so confirm the specifics for your site with the city and your design team.

Erosion Control Comes First

North Carolina requires an approved erosion and sedimentation control plan for most land disturbance over one acre, and nearly every commercial site crosses that line. Perimeter measures like silt fence and construction entrances typically go in before or alongside clearing, depending on the plan sequence. We coordinate with your erosion control contractor so the order of work matches the approved plan, and we keep our equipment and traffic inside the controls. Creek buffers under Mecklenburg County's S.W.I.M. rules also need to be respected, so confirm any buffer limits with the county.

Coordinating With Your Grading Sub

We don't grade. On a commercial site that's a feature, not a gap: we clear, and your grading contractor shapes the site. We'll talk directly with your grading sub about stump removal, whether they want the topsoil left undisturbed, where they want material staged, and the date they plan to mobilize. That conversation up front prevents the classic problem of a grader showing up to a site that's cleared but not cleared the way they needed it.

Debris, Mulch, and Haul-Off

Cleared material on a commercial site can be mulched in place, stockpiled, or hauled, depending on the plans and what your grading sub needs. Mulch is useful for erosion control in some areas and a problem in structural fill zones, so we sort that out before we start. Open burning is heavily restricted in Mecklenburg County, so burn piles aren't part of our approach on any job.

Questions

Charlotte Commercial Land Clearing FAQs

Do you work as a subcontractor to general contractors and site contractors?

Yes. Most of our commercial work is as a clearing sub. We fit into your schedule, follow your site rules, and report to your superintendent or project manager.

Can you provide a certificate of insurance before we sign?

Yes. We provide COIs to your requirements, including additional insured wording if your contract calls for it. Send us the requirements with your bid request.

Do you handle grading as part of commercial clearing?

No. We clear and hand off. Your grading contractor handles cut, fill, and final shaping. We coordinate directly with them on stumps, topsoil, and timing so the handoff is seamless.

Can you price a job from the plan set?

We can build a budget number from the plans, but we always walk the site before giving a firm price. Tree size, density, and access often look different on the ground than on paper.

How do you protect tree-save areas on a commercial site?

Protected areas should be fenced before clearing starts, and our equipment stays outside the fence the whole time, including for staging and turning. If fencing is missing or damaged, we stop and notify the site lead.

Does Charlotte's tree ordinance apply to my project?

Charlotte's tree ordinance applies mainly to commercial and new development, so it often does. Your design team and the city are the best sources for the specific tree-save and buffer requirements on your site.

Do we need an erosion control plan before clearing?

If the project disturbs more than one acre, North Carolina generally requires an approved erosion and sedimentation control plan before land disturbance begins. Confirm the requirements and any local permits with the city or county.

Can you clear in phases to match our construction schedule?

Yes. Phased clearing is common on subdivisions and larger commercial sites. We'll clear each phase to its approved limits when your schedule calls for it.

What do you do with the cleared trees and brush?

It depends on the site. Material can be mulched, stockpiled, or hauled off. We'll agree on the approach with you and your grading sub before we start, since mulch isn't wanted in structural fill areas.

How quickly can you mobilize?

It depends on our schedule and the size of your site. Call as soon as you have a target date and we'll give you an honest answer about when we can start and how long the clearing will take.
