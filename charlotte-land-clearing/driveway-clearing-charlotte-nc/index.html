Driveway Know-How

Clearing a New Driveway the Right Way in Charlotte

A New Route, Not an Old Corridor

Driveway clearing is about creating a new way in: the path from the road to a future house, barn, or building site that doesn't exist yet. That's a different job from maintaining an existing easement or utility corridor. With a new drive, the route itself is a decision, and getting it right now saves years of washouts, tight turns, and frustrated delivery drivers.

Laying Out the Route

The shortest line from the road to the house is rarely the best one. We look at where the ground is steep, where water runs during a storm, which big trees could frame the drive instead of being cut, and where the drive meets the road with good sight lines. On many Charlotte-area parcels, a gentle curve around a slope or a wet spot makes a drive cheaper to build and easier to maintain. If you have a site plan or a builder's preferred route, we'll start from that and point out anything we'd adjust.

Width for Trucks, Not Just Cars

A finished driveway for a car can be fairly narrow, but construction traffic is another story. Concrete trucks, lumber deliveries, well rigs, and equipment trailers need room to pass without scraping trees or sliding off the edge. We clear wider than the finished drive surface, with extra room on curves and at the house end for turning. Your builder may have a preferred width, and we'll clear to it. Overhanging limbs get trimmed back too, so tall trucks don't tear them down later.

Stumps Along the Drive Line

Stumps left under a driveway rot over time and leave soft spots, dips, and cracks. Along the drive line, we grind stumps out or remove them entirely, depending on what your grading or paving contractor wants underneath. Outside the drive surface, stumps can often be cut low and left. We'll set the approach in your written scope so your next contractor doesn't run into surprises.

Slopes, Drainage, and Red Clay

Charlotte's red clay turns slick when wet and washes fast on a slope once the roots and leaf litter are gone. A drive running straight down a hill can become a gully after a few heavy storms. During layout, we look for routes that follow the contour where possible and note spots where your grading contractor will need to plan for culverts or water diversion. We also avoid clearing a long corridor and leaving it bare for weeks. Timing the clearing close to when the next contractor starts keeps the clay from washing out.

Where Our Work Ends

We clear the route and remove stumps along the drive line. We don't do grading, base stone, culverts, or paving. Once the corridor is cleared, it's handed off to your grading or driveway contractor to shape and surface. If your drive crosses a creek, the crossing may need permits and is subject to stream buffer rules, so confirm with the city or county before we clear near the water.

Questions

Charlotte Driveway Clearing FAQs

How wide do you clear for a new driveway?

Wider than the finished drive surface, so construction trucks can pass and turn safely, with extra room on curves. Your builder may specify a width, and we'll clear to it. Exact dimensions are confirmed at the walk-through.

Do you remove the stumps along the driveway?

Yes. Stumps in the drive line are ground out or fully removed, depending on what your grading or paving contractor wants. Leftover stumps under a drive rot and cause soft spots later.

Do you grade, add stone, or pave the driveway?

No. We clear the route and handle the stumps along it, then hand it off to your grading or driveway contractor for shaping, base stone, and surfacing.

Can you help decide where the driveway should go?

Yes. We walk possible routes with you and look at slope, drainage, keeper trees, and the road connection, then flag the route that makes the most sense.

Is driveway clearing the same as right-of-way clearing?

No. Driveway clearing creates a new route to a home or building site. Right-of-way clearing maintains or reopens existing easements and corridors.

My driveway route crosses a creek. Is that a problem?

It can be. Creek crossings may need permits, and stream buffer rules limit clearing near many streams. Confirm the requirements with the city or county before we clear near the water.

Can you clear a long driveway on acreage?

Yes. Long lanes through wooded acreage are common for us. We stake the full route and clear it in one mobilization when possible.

Will clearing the drive route cause erosion?

Bare clay on a slope can wash, so we time the clearing close to when your grading contractor starts and point out spots where runoff will need to be managed.

Can you save trees along the driveway?

Often, yes. A curve around a big oak can turn a plain driveway into a beautiful one. We'll flag trees worth keeping and lay out the route to work with them where possible.
