Storm Cleanup Know-How

Cleaning Up After a Storm in Charlotte

What Charlotte Storms Leave Behind

Charlotte's tree canopy is one of the city's best features, and it's also why storms here do so much damage. Tall loblolly pines snap or uproot in saturated clay, water oaks drop heavy limbs, and ice storms bend and break whole stands of younger trees. Afterward, most properties have some mix of trees down across a yard or fence, limbs scattered everywhere, and a wooded back section that's now impassable.

Safety First: Power Lines and Trees Under Tension

If a tree or limb is touching a power line, stay away from it and call Duke Energy before anyone else. We don't work on or near energized lines; that belongs to the utility. Storm-downed trees also carry hidden tension. A trunk pinned under another tree, or bent over and still rooted, can spring back hard when it's cut. That's why the first step on every storm job is reading the wood before cutting it, not rushing in with a saw.

Trees on Roofs and Hung-Up Limbs

Our work is ground-based: trees and limbs that are down, or close enough to the ground to be handled safely with our equipment. A tree resting on a roof, or a large limb hung up high in another tree's crown, usually needs a climbing crew or crane first. We'll tell you honestly when that's the case. Once those pieces are on the ground, we can clear the rest.

Yards, Fence Lines, and Wooded Acreage

Storm debris doesn't stay in one place. In a neighborhood yard, the priority is getting trunks off the lawn and fence without tearing up the turf. On acreage outside the city, it's often reopening a driveway, a farm road, or a fence line so livestock stay in. In the woods, a storm can leave a jackstraw pile of blowdowns that makes the land unusable. We plan each area around what you need first and work outward from there.

What Happens to the Debris

In wooded and rural areas, mulching storm debris in place is usually the cleanest answer. It covers bare soil, avoids burn piles, and saves hauling. In a manicured yard, a thick mulch layer may not be what you want. We'll talk through the finish for each area and put it in the written scope. Charlotte's curbside yard waste pickup has size and volume limits that a downed tree typically exceeds, so confirm current rules with the city before you pile anything at the curb.

Insurance, HOAs, and Records

If you're filing a claim, photograph the damage before anything is moved. We can give you a written scope and price for your records and provide a certificate of insurance if your insurer, HOA, or property manager asks for one. What a policy covers is between you and your insurer, so check with your agent about coverage before work begins.

Questions

Charlotte Storm Debris & Downed Tree Cleanup FAQs

A tree is touching a power line. Can you remove it?

No. Stay clear and call Duke Energy first. Work on or near energized lines belongs to the utility. Once the line is safe, we can clear the rest of the debris.

How quickly can you come out after a storm?

Call us as soon as the weather passes and we'll schedule a walk-through as quickly as we can. After a major storm, demand is high across the region, so we'll give you an honest time frame on the phone.

Can you remove a tree that fell on my house?

A tree resting on a roof usually needs a climbing crew or crane to lift it off safely. Once it's on the ground, we can cut it up and clear it along with the rest of the debris.

Will you help with my insurance claim?

We can provide a written scope and price for your records and a certificate of insurance if needed. Coverage questions are between you and your insurer, so check with your agent.

What do you do with the downed trees and limbs?

In wooded and rural areas, most debris is mulched in place. In yards, we'll agree on the finish for each area and put it in the written scope.

Can I just put the debris at the curb?

Charlotte's curbside yard waste pickup has size and volume limits that a downed tree usually exceeds. Confirm current rules with the city before piling anything at the street.

Can you reopen a blocked driveway or farm road?

Yes. Reopening access is often the first priority on rural and acreage properties, and we'll start there before moving to the rest of the cleanup.

Do you grind the stumps of trees that blew over?

Uprooted trees leave a root plate and a hole rather than a stump. We can cut the trunk free and talk through options for the root plate. Stump grinding and full stump removal are available for trees that snapped off.

Can you clear storm damage deep in the woods?

Yes. Blowdowns in wooded acreage are a good fit for our tracked equipment. We'll prioritize trails, fence lines, and access first, then the rest as needed.
