Local Know-How

Clearing Land for Homes, Barns & Acreage in Waxhaw

Custom Home Lots Along NC-16, Kensington Road & Marvin Borders

Between Providence Road South (NC-16), Kensington Road, and the Marvin and Weddington borders, many custom homes go up on one- to five-acre wooded parcels. Clearing the entire lot from boundary to boundary strips away privacy and shade. Land Clearing Charlotte flags the building envelope, septic field area, and driveway with you first, clears inside those limits, and leaves a healthy stand of oaks, poplars, and pines around the perimeter.

Horse Properties, Barn Sites & Choosing the Right Clearing Method

Out along New Town Road, Cuthbertson Road, and Crane Road, land clearing often means opening up a home site plus a barn footprint, trailer turnaround, and future paddock space. Matching the clearing method to each part of the parcel keeps costs down: full tree and stump removal is used inside the house pad, barn footprint, and driveway where bare, stable ground is required for foundations and stone, while forestry mulching in place shreds brush and saplings across surrounding acreage, trails, and pasture edges without haul-off bills. We do not perform finish grading, so we leave the footprint clean and ready for your grading contractor.

Larger Tracts South of NC-75 Toward Cane Creek & JAARS

South of downtown Waxhaw toward Cane Creek Park, Jaars Road, and Brady Road, tracts run deeper and driveways frequently stretch several hundred feet through the woods. Opening a long access road early lets well drillers, septic evaluators, and builders get trucks onto the property without getting stuck in Union County clay.

Twelve Mile Creek, Cane Creek & Watershed Buffers

Western Union County drains into Twelve Mile Creek, Waxhaw Creek, and the protected Cane Creek water-supply watershed. Both the Town of Waxhaw Unified Development Ordinance (UDO) and the Union County Development Regulation enforce undisturbed stream buffers and floodway restrictions along perennial and intermittent waterways. During our walk-through we note any creeks, springs, or wet draws and recommend confirming parcel-specific buffer rules with the Town of Waxhaw Planning & Inspections Department or Union County Planning before clearing begins.

Waxhaw Permits, the One-Acre Rule & Per-Acre Costs

Inside town limits, the Town of Waxhaw UDO regulates tree preservation, specimen tree save, and perimeter buffers on development sites, while estate communities such as Providence Downs South, MillBridge, and Lawson require HOA architectural approval. Under North Carolina's Sedimentation Pollution Control Act of 1973 (G.S. 113A-57) administered by NCDEQ, an approved Erosion and Sedimentation Control Plan is required before disturbing one acre or more (including individual lots within a larger common plan of development that exceeds one acre cumulatively). Land clearing in Waxhaw typically ranges from $2,000 to $8,000 per acre depending on site conditions, with stump grinding and full stump removal quoted separately. Four factors drive where a parcel falls in that range: thick brush, the amount of tree removal involving trees thicker than 8 inches, steep banks or hilly terrain, and rocks that equipment must work around.

Waxhaw Land Clearing FAQs

How much does land clearing cost per acre in Waxhaw, NC?

Land clearing in Waxhaw typically costs $2,000 to $8,000 per acre depending on property conditions, with stump grinding and full stump removal quoted separately. The main factors that affect where your parcel lands in that range are thick brush, the amount of tree removal involving trees thicker than 8 inches, steep banks or hilly terrain, and rocks that equipment must navigate around. We provide a firm written price after a free on-site walk-through.

Do I need a permit to clear a home site in Waxhaw, NC?

Requirements depend on whether your parcel is inside Town of Waxhaw limits (governed by the Town of Waxhaw Unified Development Ordinance tree-save and buffer rules) or unincorporated Union County (Union County Development Regulation), as well as whether your lot borders Waxhaw Creek, Twelve Mile Creek, or the Cane Creek watershed. Under North Carolina's Sedimentation Pollution Control Act (G.S. 113A-57), disturbing one acre or more (or a smaller lot within a common subdivision plan exceeding one acre) requires an approved Erosion and Sedimentation Control Plan through NCDEQ. Confirm requirements with the Town of Waxhaw or Union County before starting.

How does forestry mulching compare with full land clearing on a Waxhaw tract?

Forestry mulching shreds brush and saplings in place into a protective ground cover with no haul-off expense, making it ideal for trails, pasture reclamation, and wooded buffers. Full land clearing removes trees and stumps inside structural footprints (house pads, barns, driveways) where buried wood cannot remain.

Can you clear for both a house site and a future horse pasture?

Yes. We often clear the house and barn footprint completely while mulching or selectively opening the pasture area, and we can phase the work across multiple visits.

How do you handle stumps where the house foundation and driveway will go?

Stump work is quoted separately and can involve full stump removal (recommended under foundations, slabs, and driveways) or grinding stumps down and leaving the remains where requested on non-structural ground.

Do you grade the house site after clearing?

No. We handle the tree, brush, and stump clearing and leave the envelope ready for your builder or grading contractor to cut and shape.

Can you clear a long driveway through woods off NC-75 or Crane Road?

Yes. Opening the driveway corridor first is often the best way to give surveyors, soil scientists, well drillers, and builders clear access.

Can you help with HOA tree-save submittals in Waxhaw neighborhoods?

Yes. During our walk-through we ribbon the clearing limits and keeper trees so you can photograph everything for your HOA architectural review board, and we supply our Certificate of Insurance on request.
